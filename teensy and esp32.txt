Both the ESP32 and Teensy are excellent, high-performance choices for interfacing with HC-12 wireless modules, but
the "better" option depends on whether your project requires built-in connectivity (Wi-Fi/Bluetooth) or extreme raw processing speed. 

    Choose the ESP32 if: You need Wi-Fi/Bluetooth, low power consumption, or a cheaper, all-in-one IoT solution.
    Choose the Teensy if: You need maximum CPU power (600MHz), advanced signal processing, or a very large number of I/O pins, and do not need on-board Wi-Fi.

Why Choose ESP32
The ESP32 is generally considered superior for most DIYers and IoT projects using the HC-12 because of its built-in wireless capabilities and low cost. 

    Ease of Integration: You can use Hardware Serial2 (Pins 16/17) to communicate with the HC-12 while using the USB port for debugging.
    Versatility: You can use the HC-12 for long-range 433MHz communication and simultaneously use Wi-Fi or Bluetooth for local monitoring or data logging.
    Low Power: It offers excellent deep-sleep modes for battery-powered sensor nodes.

Why Choose Teensy
The Teensy 4.0/4.1 is better suited for specialized, high-performance applications where 433MHz is just one part of a larger, complex system. 

    Raw Power: The 600 MHz ARM Cortex-M7 processor handles immense computational tasks, such as high-speed data sampling before transmitting it over the HC-12.
    Performance: If your project involves complex real-time control, robotics, or professional audio processing alongside the HC-12, the Teensy is superior.
    Pin Capabilities: Teensy 4.1 offers extensive I/O, built-in SD card slot, and Ethernet capabilities. 

Essential Tips for HC-12
Regardless of your choice, the HC-12 works best with a dedicated power supply (3.2V–5.5V) and a 10uF–1000uF capacitor between VCC and GND, as it can draw up to 100mA during transmission. 

    ESP32 Connection: Connect HC-12 VCC to 3.3V, TXD to RX2 (16), RXD to TX2 (17), and SET to a digital pin.
    Teensy Connection: Similar to ESP32, use UART/Hardware Serial, but ensure the HC-12 voltage does not exceed 3.3V on the RX/TX lines to avoid damaging the Teensy.
